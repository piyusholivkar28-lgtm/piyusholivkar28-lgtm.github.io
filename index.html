Position

I study the human body as a physical system—governed by mechanics, transport and dynamical structure.

My work sits between mechanical engineering and physiology. The common thread is not one organ or disease, but a way of asking questions: what is the simplest physically defensible model that exposes the mechanism?

The long-term aim is mathematical space physiology: engineering tools that explain, predict and eventually help manage human adaptation during long-duration missions.

Research

Five systems. One mechanistic language.

Mechanics, transport and dynamical systems used to expose physiological and fluid-mechanical mechanisms.

  • 01

    Sperm motility from microscopy to simulation

    Measured flagellar curvature, FCM flow reconstruction and hydrodynamic interaction rules.
  • 02

    Cerebral haemodynamics in altered gravity

    Patient-specific 3D carotid CFD with RCR boundary conditions and cerebral autoregulation.
  • 03

    Circadian–endocrine synchronization

    SCN–adrenal phase locking, delay, noise and early-warning signals.
  • 04

    Partial-gravity skeletal-muscle response

    ISS mouse phenotypes, gravity-response identifiability and future experimental design.
  • 05

    Centrifugal instability in toroidal Couette flow

    Critical Reynolds number and eigenmode localization between rotating concentric tori.

01 — About

Engineering the physiology problem from first principles.

My research trajectory began in mechanical engineering and thermal-fluid systems, then moved toward biofluid mechanics and mechanistic modeling of physiological systems. The common thread is not a single organ or disease; it is the use of physical laws and mathematical structure to explain how biological systems behave.

I am currently a PhD Scholar in the Department of Applied Mechanics and Biomedical Engineering at IIT Madras, working with the CoPhe Lab. My present work is centered on sperm mechanics, microhydrodynamics and computational modeling, while parallel research interests extend to cardiovascular, endocrine/circadian and metabolic physiology.

“Build the simplest physically defensible model that reveals the mechanism before adding complexity.”

04 — Sperm / microhydrodynamics

From observed flagellar motion to hydrodynamic mechanism.

A sperm cell is a strongly coupled mechanics–fluid system. The observed flagellar waveform is the result of internal actuation, elastic response and viscous loading—not simply a prescribed curve. My PhD work uses computational microhydrodynamics to connect kinematics, force generation, fluid disturbance and swimmer interaction.

μ∇²u − ∇p + f = 0 ∇·u = 0 κ(s,t) ↔ fluid loading

05 — Mathematical physiology

Reduced models as tools for mechanism, not just curve fitting.

Complex physiological systems often contain feedback, time scales, delays and competing control mechanisms. I use reduced mathematical models to isolate which mechanisms can explain a response, where stability is lost and which parameters are actually identifiable.

06 — Space physiology

Future research direction

Human adaptation to extreme environments and long-duration spaceflight.

My long-term goal is to develop mechanistic and computational models that connect altered physical environments to physiological adaptation across organ systems. Spaceflight is a natural test case because microgravity perturbs transport, loading, fluid distribution, feedback control and biological rhythms simultaneously.

The intended direction is explicitly engineering-driven: start from the governing physics, construct reduced or multiscale physiological models, test identifiability and use simulation to determine which mechanisms can plausibly explain observed adaptation.
